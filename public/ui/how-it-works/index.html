STEP 1 / 6 · LEARN

First, it learns what the real ShopNova looks like.

BrandWatch opens shopnova.com once and keeps a fingerprint: the favicon's hash, a picture of the homepage, its colours, its login form and the words a phisher would copy.

  • AI reads the site and writes the Google searches an attacker would target.

Every suspect is later compared against this fingerprint, not against a guess.

STEP 2 / 6 · FIND

Then it hunts for look-alikes — before anyone reports them.

Typo domains on 1,000+ endings, new HTTPS certificates as they are issued, yesterday's registrations and Google results — nothing has to be on a blocklist first.

  • Social: Facebook pages, Instagram profiles and ads under the brand's name, read by an AI.
  • AI assistants: ChatGPT and Gemini are asked what a customer asks; a phone or e-mail that is not on the brand's site becomes a finding.

Found before anyone reports it — the brand's own sites and big platforms are dropped first.

STEP 3 / 6 · VISIT

It visits every site the way a victim would.

Many phishing kits hide from security scanners: a harmless page for datacenter IPs, the fake login only for real people. So BrandWatch also visits as a real person.

  • A real Chrome: its User-Agent, Accept-Language, client hints, language and time zone all agree — no "HeadlessChrome" giveaway.
  • A real address: through a residential or a mobile 4G / 5G proxy (or both) in the brand's country, as a desktop and / or a phone.
  • A locked-down browser that can't reach internal systems; everything it sees is kept as evidence nobody can edit.

The score uses what a victim sees, not what the kit chose to show a scanner.

STEP 4 / 6 · SCORE

It scores the site — and shows its work.

50 signals in 7 capped groups, each worth fixed points: does the address use the brand, does the page look the same, does it ask for a password, how new is it, is it hiding.

  • The same evidence always gives the same score; AI is a second opinion and never changes the number.

No black box: every point traces back to a piece of evidence.

STEP 5 / 6 · DECIDE & MAP

A person decides. Then it maps the attacker.

Nothing is confirmed automatically: an analyst reads the evidence and presses Confirm. From that one site, BrandWatch follows what the attacker reused — server, certificate, favicon, tracking code, owner — to the rest of the operation.

  • Neighbours that only share a cheap server are dropped as noise, with the reason written down.

One fake site leads to the whole operation, and every indicator exports to a SIEM as STIX 2.1.

STEP 6 / 6 · TAKE DOWN

Finally, it drafts the takedown.

A request backed by the evidence, addressed to the people who can pull the site: the registrar, the hosting provider and the CDN — contacts found automatically.

  • 26 report portals (browsers, anti-virus, blocklists) so the site is blocked for everyone while the takedown is pending.
  • A fake Facebook / Instagram ad or page gets Meta's report flow and a ready-to-paste report.

BrandWatch never sends anything by itself: a person reviews and sends, and every step lands in the audit log.

SSRF guard & sandbox

Every fetch: validate the URL → resolve A/AAAA → fail closed on 24 blocked networks (private, loopback, cloud metadata) → connect pinned to the validated IP with Host + SNI → re-validate every redirect hop (≤ 5) → size ≤ 5.0 MB, timeout 15.0 s.

The same policy gates every Chromium sub-request. Limits are frozen in code — no brand, workspace or env var can change them.

Immutable evidence

10 append-only tables: database triggers reject UPDATE and DELETE — even for an admin, on SQLite and PostgreSQL. Artifacts are content-addressed: the SHA-256 is the file name.

Each finding links its evidence with a role (primary, supporting, contradicting, context): a chain of custody for a takedown or a court.

Provider resilience

Every external call goes through one wrapper: TTL cache → budget check → circuit breaker → token bucket → retry with backoff (honouring Retry-After) → the next provider in the fallback chain.

A bad key disables the provider for the job instead of failing 500 calls; "unknown" is never treated as clean.

Glossary· every “?” on the site links here
Baseline
The fingerprint of the brand's real site — favicon hash, screenshot hash, colours, login pages, keywords. Every suspect is compared against it.
Candidate / watched domain
Any domain discovered for a brand. It lives on the Watchlist until it scores high enough to become a finding, expires, or is dismissed.
Finding
A candidate that scored Medium or higher. An analyst confirms it as phishing or dismisses it; nothing happens automatically.
Signal
One piece of evidence (e.g. 'identical favicon'). 50 signals in 7 groups; each adds points.
Group cap
Each group can add at most its cap, so ten weak visual hints never outweigh one strong behaviour signal.
Risk score
How dangerous the site is, 0–100. Medium ≥ 25, High ≥ 50, Critical ≥ 75. Combination rules can raise the severity.
Confidence
How complete and consistent the evidence is (breadth, completeness, provider agreement) — separate from risk.
Cloaking
A kit that shows a harmless decoy to scanners and the phishing page to victims. BrandWatch fetches as a bot, as a victim link, from a search referer and from a home IP.
Detection track
Light, Standard or Deep: which discovery streams run, how many sites are visited, how often the watchlist is re-checked.
Attack infrastructure
The map built from one confirmed site: every server, certificate and sister domain it shares, triaged so reverse-IP noise is excluded. The pivot is budgeted (at most 2 hops, 100 nodes, a fixed number of API calls) and those limits are frozen in code.
Advisory opinions
An AI model and a trained classifier each give a second opinion on Medium+ findings. The page text is passed as untrusted input and the answer must match a strict JSON schema. Shown for context only: the deterministic score never depends on them.
IOC
Indicator of compromise: a domain, IP, certificate hash or tracking ID a firewall / SIEM can block. Exported as CSV, JSON or STIX 2.1.
Takedown
The request to remove a site. Draft → review → approved → sent → taken down; sent to the registrar, the hosting provider and the CDN, and reported to blocklists.
Evidence
Every screenshot, HTML page, DNS / WHOIS answer and reputation lookup, stored append-only with its SHA-256 — the database rejects edits and deletes.
Discovery stream
One way of finding look-alikes: look-alike names (dnstwist), new certificates (CT), newly registered domains (NRD), Google search, the urlscan.io archive, social media, ads. A track decides which streams run.
dnstwist
Generates the names a phisher would register — typos, swapped letters, other TLDs, look-alike Unicode — and checks in DNS which of them exist.
Certificate transparency (CT)
Every HTTPS certificate is published in public logs within minutes; a new certificate carrying the brand's name is often the first sign of a kit.
Newly registered domains (NRD)
Yesterday's list of every newly registered domain (about 70,000 a day), searched for the brand's name.
urlscan.io
A public archive of scanned web pages: past scans that mention the brand, and a screenshot of a site that blocks BrandWatch.
Home / mobile IP probe
A visit through a home (residential) or 4G/5G address, for kits that show the real page only to people, never to a datacentre. Needs a proxy and a GB budget.
STIX 2.1
The standard file format security tools exchange threat indicators in; the infrastructure map's indicators can be exported as STIX for a SIEM or a firewall.
Golden dataset
Recorded, hand-labelled scenarios (phishing, cloaked kits, scam shops, legitimate sites) the real pipeline is run on in every build; precision and recall are measured on them.
TLD set
Which top-level domains dnstwist tries: common (.com .net …), popular (about 100) or all (about 1,280).
Vertical
The kind of business ("footwear and apparel", "crypto exchange") — read by the AI from the official site, used to pick the right Google queries.
Who sells it
Only the brand (a service, an app, a bank) or other shops too: then scam shops advertising its products are hunted as well.
Demo mode
Recorded scenarios replace every external service: the full pipeline runs deterministically, offline, at $0.
